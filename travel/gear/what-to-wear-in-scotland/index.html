What to wear in Scotland, month by month

Scotland's weather changes fast, and it rains in every month. The trick isn't one big coat, it's layers you can add and peel off, and a waterproof for everyone. Here's what the numbers say and what we'd pack for adults and kids. Facts checked October 2026, with sources at the end.

Scotland's weather by month (Edinburgh)

These are the Met Office's 1991–2020 long-term averages for Edinburgh (Royal Botanic Garden). "Rainy days" means days with at least 1 mm of rain. Highlands and west-coast weather is often wetter and windier than Edinburgh, and the hills are colder, so if you're heading to Skye or Glencoe, pack for the wetter end.

The layering system that works

  • Base layer: something that dries fast and doesn't smell after a day: merino wool or a synthetic. Skip cotton for walks; it stays wet.
  • Mid layer: a fleece or light insulated jacket.
  • Outer layer: a waterproof jacket with a hood. For kids, add waterproof trousers so they can sit on wet grass and splash in puddles.
  • Feet: waterproof shoes or boots, wool socks, and wellies for small kids.

The classic: a Barbour waxed jacket

If you want the jacket you'll see all over rural Scotland, it's a Barbour. Waxed cotton handles drizzle and wind well and looks better with age, though Orvis notes most Barbour waxed jackets are weather-resistant rather than fully waterproof, and they're heavier than a shell. It's more of a "buy once, wear for years" piece than packing gear. Barbour waxed jackets cost about $415 to $450 at US retailers when we checked on 22 October 2026.

A quick Scotland packing checklist

  • Waterproof jacket for every person (and waterproof trousers for kids)
  • A fleece or warm mid layer each
  • Quick-dry or merino base layers; fewer cotton T-shirts
  • Waterproof shoes or boots, wellies for small kids, wool socks
  • Hat and gloves outside summer; sun hat and suncream in summer
  • Midge repellent and a head net (late May to September, west Highlands)
  • UK plug adapters (type G)

Questions

Do I need a waterproof jacket in Scotland in summer?

Yes. July and August are the warmest months in Edinburgh, with average highs around 19 °C (66 °F), but they're also among the wettest: about 72 mm of rain and 10 to 11 rainy days a month (1991–2020 Met Office averages). A light, packable waterproof for every person is the single most useful thing you can bring.

What should kids wear in Scotland?

Layers. A thin base layer, a fleece and a waterproof jacket let you add or remove warmth as the day changes. Add waterproof trousers and wellies (rubber boots) for anything muddy, and pack a spare set of clothes in the car.

Is a waxed jacket like a Barbour good for Scotland?

It's a classic for a reason: waxed cotton sheds rain and wind well. Retailers like Orvis describe most Barbour waxed jackets as weather-resistant rather than fully waterproof, and they're heavy to pack. For a heavy downpour or a hike, a modern waterproof shell is lighter and drier.

When is the driest time to visit Edinburgh?

On the 1991–2020 Met Office averages, April is the driest month in Edinburgh (about 41 mm of rain over 8 to 9 rainy days), and October is the wettest (about 76 mm). It can rain in any month, so pack a waterproof whenever you go.

Should we bring wellies or hiking boots?

For young kids, wellies: they're easy to get on and off and you can splash through anything. Adults doing real walks are better off in waterproof walking shoes or boots. For city days in Edinburgh, comfortable waterproof shoes are enough.
