How to budget by paycheck

A monthly budget says you earn $3,200 and spend $2,900, so you're fine. Then rent, the car payment and insurance all land in the first week, and you're not fine. Budgeting by paycheck fixes that by planning money in the order it actually arrives.

  1. List your paydays for the next month, using your take-home pay, not your salary.
  2. List your bills with their due dates. Your bank or card app shows them. Put things you spend every pay period, like groceries, gas and savings, on every paycheck.
  3. Assign each bill to the last paycheck before it's due. That's what the calculator does. Rent due on the 1st comes out of the check from the end of the previous month.
  4. Look at what's left on each check. That's your spending money until the next payday. If one check goes negative, move a bill to an earlier check or ask the company to change the due date.

Reading your results

Each card is one paycheck: the date, which bills it pays and their due dates, and what's left over. A red card means that check can't cover its bills. When that happens, the calculator looks for bills it can pay earlier from a check that has room. It never moves a bill later, so nothing will be late. You'll see the suggested changes and a balanced version of the plan.

If you're paid every week or every two weeks, switch the plan to 2 or 3 months. You'll see why some checks feel bigger than others, and you'll spot the months with an extra paycheck coming.

What to do with what's left

The "left" number on each card isn't extra money. It's what you have for everything that isn't a bill: eating out, gifts, the kids' school things, the random Target run. If one check leaves $600 and the next leaves $90, plan your bigger spending around the $600 week, and keep the $90 week quiet. When every check has a positive number, you can start pointing some of it at a sinking fund or extra debt payments.

Tips that make it work

  • Leave a small cushion on every check, even $50, for the bill you forgot or the price that went up.
  • Pay savings first. Put it on every paycheck as a bill, so it isn't whatever's left at the end.
  • Move due dates once. One phone call to the car lender or the phone company can permanently balance your checks.
  • Use a separate bills account if you can. Move each check's bill total there on payday, and what stays in checking is yours to spend.

Questions

What does budgeting by paycheck mean?

Instead of one monthly budget, you plan each paycheck on its own: which bills it pays, what goes to savings, and what's left to spend until the next payday. It's the easiest way to stop the 'rent week' crunch, when one check has to cover almost everything.

Which paycheck should pay which bill?

The usual rule, and the one this calculator uses, is that each bill is paid from the last paycheck that arrives on or before its due date. A bill due on the 1st is paid from the check you get at the end of the month before. If you want a cushion, set 'pay bills early' to 2 or 3 days.

What if one paycheck can't cover its bills?

The calculator flags it and suggests moving one or more bills to an earlier paycheck that has room left. Paying early is always allowed, so that's often enough to balance it. If it isn't, many companies will change a due date if you call and ask, which is often the easiest permanent fix.

What about months with three paychecks?

If you're paid every week or every two weeks, a few months a year have an extra paycheck. Set the plan to 2 or 3 months to see them. Because your monthly bills are already covered, an extra check is a great time to build savings or pay down debt.

Does it handle paydays that fall on a weekend?

It uses the dates you enter. Many employers pay on the Friday before a weekend or holiday payday. If yours does, use that date. Paying a bill a day or two early never hurts.
